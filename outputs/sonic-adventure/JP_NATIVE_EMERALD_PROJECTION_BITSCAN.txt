Leading-zero instruction for Emerald Coast projection

The Windows diagnostic builder accepts --bit-scan. With that option it
checks the pinned SH-4 header hash, generates a private copy with one
replacement, and recompiles the projection object. The shipped vendor
header stays byte-identical. The replacement finds the highest set bit
of the nonzero dot-product magnitude in one operation. This preserves the
exponent and rounding inputs. Other linked objects keep their prior code.

Use the same inputs and commands as JP_NATIVE_EMERALD_PROJECTION_XYZ.txt
with --bit-scan on the builder and fresh private output paths.

The rebuilt projection passed 1,554 comparisons across 192 original
calls and the Windows scene passed 840 checks. All eight captures
matched the previous build by SHA256. Across 48 frames, the two prior
XYZ runs took 10.86 and 10.02 seconds inside the projection loop. The two
bit-scan experiments took 10.22 and 9.67 seconds. The first experiment used
the equivalent change directly in a temporary header edit. These timings
describe the diagnostic workload. Overall completion is about 10 percent.

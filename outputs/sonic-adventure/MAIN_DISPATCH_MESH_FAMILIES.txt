What changed
A separate strict linked build now verifies all nine recovered material families
together in the actual native mesh/material loop. Three new scripts build, verify
and register this integration, with a separate lifetime regression script. The
aggregate workflow runs these in dependency order. Existing recovered C and
historical proof/DLL files remain unchanged. This task adds no recovered routines.

The nine families are flat material colour, per-vertex material lighting, signed
UV/per-vertex lighting, generated environment UV/per-vertex lighting, normalized
face material lighting, signed UV/normalized face factor, signed UV/constant
factor, environment UV/normalized face factor and environment UV/constant factor.
Each has ordinary/clipped parents, a near clipper and two alternate strip helpers;
together with the reused normalizer these are 46 exercised native entry targets.

New real-caller evidence
Earlier face-family guides documented controlled body tests that seeded R12,
R14 and R13 with cache pointers. Those tests did not establish a game caller ABI.
The new tests start from native mesh/material dispatch, without register seeding,
and observe both original execution and native call-boundary state.

At the first near-crossing non-alternate 8000-topology group, the face family
reads at 8c61bca2, the signed-UV face family at 8c61cdc6 and the environment-face
family at 8c61ef72. The actual caller supplies:
  R12 = segment record, 8cc90000 in the constructed fixture
  R14 = renderer state, 8c8ea3a0
  R13 = material record, 8cca0000
These are valid RAM addresses but are not the three indexed vertex-cache records.
Original instructions reinterpret their first three words as normals and sum
those words before normalizing. Constructed ordinary material colour bytes include
a signaling NaN under this interpretation; the original-instruction reference and
native numerical backend propagate NaN. Tests preserve that behavior and do not
substitute a normal or colour fallback. This is not hardware NaN-payload proof.

History also matters. An ordinary three-vertex group followed by crossing indices
1,2,3 computes crossing lighting from the earlier group's pointers 0,1,2. A
four-vertex crossing group followed by another crossing group uses pointers 2,3,3
for the second group's lighting. The subsequent clipper geometry arguments are
loaded from the current index stream. Finite pre-normalizer sums are independently
checked at binary32 rounding boundaries; NaN classification is checked separately.
A fourth normal distinct from the first prevents accidental equality of old/current
face normals from hiding this effect.

These observations resolve what the tested material caller actually supplies.
They do not establish that the shipped game reaches these specific material/
8000-topology/near-crossing combinations. Treat apparent stale-pointer lighting
as preserved observed original behavior until original asset reachability is
established. Earlier controlled body proofs remain valid within their stated
scope; their seeded pointers must not be treated as a confirmed caller contract.

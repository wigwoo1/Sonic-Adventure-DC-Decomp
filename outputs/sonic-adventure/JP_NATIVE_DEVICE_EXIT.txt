Audio/overlay traces that remain in the old scoped harness are still traces;

The general exit service delegates shutdown of device-owned resources instead
of also owning their borrowed GPU/window handles. A native 64-bit token records
one terminal authority transfer on the creating thread. Rejected binds leave
the device unclaimed and the exit service unbound. After claim, ordinary device
shutdown/configure/initialize cannot close or replace those resources.
Wrong tokens, including mismatches in the high 32 bits, and foreign threads are
rejected without changing ownership. Creator-thread restrictions persist after
delegated shutdown completes; no same-process restart is implied.

Cleanup has two stages. The device first drains pending presentation without
game callbacks and stops its logical wait service. Window/GPU/video/clock stay
alive while the exit service closes input/recording, destroys tasks, releases
assets and retires frame/queue/texture/render storage. The device then finishes
its remaining cleanup. External retained frames or GPU jobs prevent completion
and preserve the remaining native owners for a genuine retry. Successfully
closed owners are never closed twice. Original task destructors run once and
cannot recursively enter exit cleanup.

Existing exit structures and classic JP_EXIT_ALL=16383 retain their ABI and
meaning. JP_EXIT_DEVICE=16384 selects the delegated device. It cannot be combined
with independently owned PRESENT/WAIT/CLOCK/WINDOW/GPU bits or raw GPU/window
handles. JP_EXIT_WITH_DEVICE=18422 selects the device and other classic owners;
its config supplies NULL GPU/window pointers. Both the new path and previous
classic mode pass complete original-logo regression suites.

actual native device initialization, original game/Ninja text, pending GPU work
and independently retained frames/jobs. It covers authority validation, normal
cleanup, pending drain without EOR game callbacks, external-owner recovery,

simulated return from the non-returning native function.
